# Identity checks and tenant referencing

**When this applies.** Somebody has to be verified before they can proceed, and
documents about them are collected, checked and stored.

## The shape it usually takes

1. Ask for the documents.
2. Chase the ones that do not arrive.
3. Check each one is what it claims to be and belongs to the person.
4. Run whatever external reference or check applies.
5. Record the outcome and when it was done.
6. Tell the person, and whoever is waiting on it.
7. Store the evidence for as long as it must be kept, and no longer.

## Where a person is usually needed

- **The decision itself.** Passing or failing somebody on a check about their
  identity, their money or their right to rent has consequences for them and a
  regulator may ask how it was reached.
- **Anything sent outside carrying the documents.** These are identity documents
  about a living person, so where they go and how they travel is the question,
  not a detail.
- **Any result that is borderline.** The whole point of the check.

## What usually goes unasked

- What is the retention period, and what deletes them at the end of it?
- Who can see the documents once stored?
- What happens on a fail: who tells the person, and in what words?

## Worth saying plainly

Collecting and chasing documents is ordinary work and automates well. Judging
them does not, and moving them around is where the risk sits.
