# Rent collection and arrears chase

**When this applies.** Money arrives from tenants on a cycle, somebody checks it
landed, and somebody chases the ones that did not.

## The shape it usually takes

1. Pull the payments expected for the period from the tenancy records.
2. Pull what actually arrived, from the bank feed or a statement.
3. Match one against the other, usually on a reference that is sometimes wrong.
4. Mark the matched ones as received.
5. Decide what to do about each shortfall.
6. Contact the tenants who are behind.
7. Tell the landlord where their money is.

## Where a person is usually needed

- **Deciding what to do about a shortfall.** A payment three days late from a
  reliable tenant and a payment three weeks late from one already in arrears are
  the same number and completely different situations.
- **Sending the arrears notice.** It goes to a person outside the business, it
  affects a tenancy, and a wrongly sent one is difficult to take back.
- **Anything that starts a formal process.** Notices have legal weight and
  timing rules, which is not somewhere to find out your matching logic was off.

## What usually goes unasked

- What counts as late: one day, or after a grace period?
- Do part payments get chased for the remainder, or left until the next cycle?
- Who is told first, the tenant or the landlord?
